# Decision models beyond classification: research brief

*2026-09-28. Four literature reviews (capacity and carving; diffusion-LM mechanics; specialisation, distillation and calibration; novelty scouting), ~120 primary sources read, plus first-hand reading of OpenJev, DiffusionGemma and JevBench. Compute constraint: an Apple M6 (32 GB) and rented GPUs only where a laptop cannot do the job.*

## 0. The hypothesis, as posed and as it survives review

**As posed.** OpenJev runs a multilingual, general-purpose 26B-A4B diffusion model as a decision model. Making the model multilingual and general leaves performance on the table and wastes parameters; an English, decision-specialised model could match or beat it far smaller.

**As it survives.** Three of the four clauses hold, one does not, and the interesting science is in a fifth thing nobody has measured.

1. *Wasted parameters*: yes, but as memory, not quality. At this scale the curse of multilinguality costs English almost nothing (X-ELM: 0.1 perplexity at 1.7B; Blevins et al. 2024); the non-English capacity sits in routed experts in the outer layers and 50–75% of experts are removable on same-shaped autoregressive MoEs with task-calibrated saliency (Bandarkar & Peng 2026; Lu et al. 2024; REAP 2025). Carving lands at 3–5B total, the same class as the models that already top the leaderboard, and does not cut latency on a bandwidth-bound MoE.
2. *Performance on the table*: yes, decisively. On JevBench v1.4.2.2 OpenJev ranks **#21 (composite 36.85; 81.8% public, 28.6% sealed)**; the leaders are **4B causal models with LoRA readouts** (Imajev-4B 67.4, Plumb-4B 65.8, decider-4b 64.1), above Jev itself (63.3). Specialisation beats size by thirty points.
3. *"~100x smaller"*: not supported. Sub-1B encoders collapse on the sealed hard tier (open-jev-deberta 12.65; laya 30.3; verdict-small 5.7), roughly 35 Intelligence points behind the 4B decoders. The in-distribution result we have been quoting (Verdict 77.1 vs Jev 72.7 on typed-decisions) is within label noise: the labels are LLM-derived with a teacher self-agreement ceiling of 0.735, and Verdict trained on that split while Jev did not. The defensible target is **4B-class decision quality at 0.4–1B**, and the gap to close is hard-tier intelligence, not throughput.
4. *English-only*: for a target below 1B the multilingual vocabulary is mostly embedding rows that cost memory, not accuracy (Ushio et al. 2023: English keeps ~70% of an XLM-R vocabulary anyway). Real but small.
5. *The unmeasured thing*: OpenJev's read is not what its README says it is. DiffusionGemma is **uniform-state diffusion, not masked diffusion** (tech report arXiv:2608.00146: forward kernel κ_t δ(x_t, x_0) + (1−κ_t)/V; confirmed independently by arXiv:2607.13013). OpenJev seeds every answer slot with a *random vocabulary token* and reads once, so one read is one Monte Carlo sample of the marginal conditioned on that noise, not the marginal; that is why it re-reads with fresh seeds when entropy exceeds 0.1. In a single denoising step the K slots are conditionally independent given the canvas (MDLM's factorisation; Mask-Predict), so the model cannot reason across questions in one read, and the diffusion-LM literature documents systematic overconfidence of entropy-derived confidence (arXiv:2607.27386: LLaDA-8B ECE 0.81 on TriviaQA). Nobody has measured any of this on OpenJev.

## 1. OpenJev, verified

- Base: `google/diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it`, a fine-tune of the Gemma 4 MoE (25.2B total, 3.8B active; 128 routed experts + 1 shared, top-8; 30 layers; 262K vocabulary; 140+ pretraining languages; image input; 256K context). Made from the autoregressive model with <10% of its token budget: SFT for bidirectional denoising, then RL with sampler distillation. It trails the autoregressive Gemma 4 by 5 MMLU-Pro points (77.6 vs 82.6) and 6.6 GPQA points.
- Architecture: an encoder prefills the prompt (state + questions) into a KV cache; a bidirectional decoder processes a 256-token canvas with cross-attention to it. "Read once, decide many" is native.
- OpenJev does no training. `engine.py` builds `head + lead + answer_text + [TURN_CLOSE] + padding`, one slot per question, fills each slot with `rng.randrange(262144)`, runs vLLM with `diffusion_max_steps=1`, `diffusion_read_only=True`, all non-slot positions pinned, `logprob_token_ids = label_ids`; softmax over the label logprobs at T=1; confidence 1 − H(p)/ln K; if any slot's entropy > 0.1, three more reads with fresh seeds, averaged. On Apple silicon the MLX backend uses `mlx_vlm` (`diffusion_prefill_cache`, `diffusion_decoder_logits`); the 4-bit conversion is ~14 GB and runs on the M6 at roughly 0.2–0.4 s per 3-question request. The router selects experts with `mx.argpartition`, so per-layer expert utilisation is hookable without forking the model.
- Reported latency (RTX PRO 6000): 27 ms for one question, 31 ms for three. No accuracy, Brier or ECE in the README.

## 3. Six findings from the literature that shape the programme

1. **A one-pass canvas read of a masked diffusion LM is exactly an encoder filling `[MASK]` tokens.** D3PM: "BERT is a one-step diffusion model"; MDLM's loss is a weighted average of MLM losses; LLaDA differs from BERT only in the masking-rate prior. Laya and Verdict do the same operation per question with option markers. DiffusionGemma breaks the equivalence only by using a random token instead of a mask.
2. **Multi-slot canvases carry position bias and interference.** The one study of multi-slot canvases in a masked DLM (Kumar 2026) found the first slot positive on 99.4% of examples and retreated to per-label scoring; batched prompting in AR LLMs moves accuracy by −3 to +12 points depending on model and task (Wang et al. 2024; MTI Bench 2024); decider-4b and kev isolate question rows precisely because reordering questions changes other answers. Never measured for typed decisions in a bidirectional model, and never separated into the one-pass (independent) and multi-step (answer-conditioned) regimes that OpenJev exposes for free.
3. **Entropy-derived confidence from diffusion LMs is overconfident**, and diffusion classifiers do not produce probabilities directly (Clark & Jaini 2023, Appendix E). The best-supported calibration recipe for one-pass models is a proper-scoring loss (CE + Brier) plus a held-out temperature per question type, giving in-domain ECE ≈ 0.01–0.03 (Desai & Durrett 2020; Kotoba's DeBERTa 0.014; Verdict 0.014). laya's RLCD did not deliver calibration by itself (raw ECE 0.213–0.466; fixed by per-type temperature), and RL with calibration rewards (RLCR, ConfTuner) has only been shown for verbalised confidence in generative models.
4. **Distribution distillation from an LLM decision teacher works and the student can out-calibrate the teacher** (Fu et al. 2023: distribution matching converges faster and lower than sample matching; Wang, Deng & Yang 2026: ECE −43%, Brier −34% with a BERT student; teacher calibration predicts student accuracy, arXiv:2508.20224). Imajev and laya already use soft targets; nobody calibrates the teacher first, and nobody has used a diffusion canvas as the teacher.
5. **Where the multilingual capacity lives**: ~1% of neurons are language-specific and English has the fewest (Tang et al. 2024); 50%-sparse language subnetworks overlap 0.65–0.98 (Foroutan et al. 2022); language-exclusive experts sit in the outer layers and are nearly disjoint from English's (Chen 2026; Zheng 2026); expert pruning for one task/language keeps quality to 50–70% removal on Qwen3-30B-A3B and Gemma-4-26B-A4B (Bandarkar & Peng 2026; "Half the Experts", Jha 2026) but never on a diffusion MoE. Task arithmetic can subtract a language's behaviour but frees no parameters.
6. **Specialised small beats general large in-distribution and loses out-of-distribution.** Fine-tuned 125–435M encoders beat GPT-4-class zero-shot by 5–70 F1 on classification (Bucher & Martini 2024; Pecher et al. 2025: ~100 labels to break even); Kotoba's DeBERTa-v3-large beats a 7B diffusion MoE on typed decisions in-domain (0.855 vs 0.835) but falls to 0.622 on held-out question families, and ModernBERT-base to 0.485. The binding constraint for a small decision model is unseen question families, not throughput.

## 5. The programme

**Phase A, laptop, $0, about two days.** OpenJev anatomy (A) and the expert census (E, census only) on the same runs.
- Data: the 231 public JevBench items (72 original + 48 easy + 111 hard) and the 2,000 typed-decisions test decisions. We cannot see the sealed tier; every public number carries the known ~50-point caveat.
- Model: `mlx-community/diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it-4bit` through OpenJev's MLX backend, instrumented at the router (`mx.argpartition`) and at `build_canvas` (slot seeds).
- Conditions: 16 seeded single reads per canvas; each question alone vs all questions in one read vs steps 2–4 vs sequential reads with earlier answers revealed; slot-order permutations; the same canvases machine-translated into three languages for the census control.
- Measurements: per-slot total variation of a single read from its own 16-read mean and the flip rate; ECE, Brier, McNemar on argmax flips between conditions; per-type reliability curves for 1 − H/ln K; experts per layer carrying 99% of routing mass on slot tokens vs state tokens, English vs translated (Jaccard); OpenJev's own accuracy/Brier/ECE on typed-decisions.
- Predictions: single reads deviate ≥0.05 TV from their mean on ≥30% of slots and averaging cuts ECE by ≥0.03; one-pass joint ≈ alone within 1 point (conditional independence) while steps>1 and sequential reads change ≥5% of answers and worsen ECE; slot order changes >5% of argmaxes; outer layers need <50% of experts for 99% mass on English canvases with English/translated Jaccard <0.4 there and >0.6 mid-stack.
- What kills it: negligible seed variance *and* no order effect *and* per-type ECE under 0.05: then OpenJev's design is sound, B loses its interference motivation and becomes purely a size/architecture question.

**Phase B, one rented GPU day, ≤$40.** Canvas-native student (B), "is diffusion necessary?" (C) and calibration-aware targets (D) on one pipeline.
- Teacher labels: OpenJev 16-read-averaged per-slot distributions on ~100k public English decisions (crh225/plumb-decisions, Mapika mixture v2, Imajev's CC-BY subset, typed-decisions train), ~40 min on a rented RTX PRO 6000/H100 via vLLM, ≈$2–5; the M6 can do it overnight instead.
- Students: ModernBERT-base (149M, M6) and ModernBERT-large (395M, ~2 H100-hours per variant, ≈$15–20 for all variants). Canvas input: state + K questions + one `[MASK]` per question, ≤512 tokens; random slot revealing during training; attention regimes as a block mask (isolation / full / late exchange); targets: gold labels, raw teacher, temperature-calibrated teacher, multi-step teacher; CE + Brier; held-out per-type temperature.
- Evaluation: JevBench public hard tier and typed-decisions, with a held-out question-family split so the result is open-schema; interference (mean absolute probability shift when 8 unrelated questions are added); conjunction calibration on composite decisions.
- Predictions: isolation matches full attention on single-question accuracy while cutting interference ≥3x; late exchange wins ≥5 points on composite decisions; the 395M student exceeds laya (hard-tier accuracy ≥0.60, ECE ≤0.10); the one-read-KD student lands within 3 points of the teacher's one-read accuracy; soft targets beat hard labels by ≥3 hard-tier points.
- What kills it: full attention within 1 point of isolation with interference already <0.02 (attention structure is irrelevant at this scale, and the paper collapses to "we fine-tuned ModernBERT on decisions"); or no target gets the 395M student past 0.50 on the hard tier (world knowledge, not specialisation, is binding, and the honest conclusion is a ~4B decoder).

**Phase C, optional, ≤$40.** Expert carving of DiffusionGemma with canvas-calibrated saliency at 25/50/75% and a 2-hour LoRA recovery, only if Phase A's census shows a knee rather than a cliff. The product is a characterisation curve (parameters, active parameters, laptop latency, Intelligence, ECE), not a smaller model.

Total rented spend across A–C: under $100, most of it optional, none of it before Phase A has reported.

## 6. What would falsify the revised hypothesis

- OpenJev scoring ≥80% with Brier ≤0.06 on typed-decisions, or no student beating the teacher's Brier: the "26B leaves calibration on the table" clause fails.
- A 395M canvas student stuck below 0.50 on the public hard tier under every target and attention regime: the size clause fails for encoders; the target moves to ~1B or a carved teacher.
- Multilingual mmBERT-base beating an English-vocabulary-trimmed copy by >2 points at equal training: the multilinguality clause fails on accuracy and holds only on memory (a $0 side experiment on the M6, worth running in Phase A).
- Single-read seed variance negligible: the random-token objection is moot and OpenJev's re-reads are wasted compute rather than a correction of a flaw.
